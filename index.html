Reflecting on Death Amidst COVID-19 and Individual Creativity

Journal of Applied Psychology

Death awareness was a very real experience during the initial peak of the COVID-19 pandemic, and it brought unexpected consequences. Using weekly panel data, we found that death reflection was positively linked to creativity, highlighting how certain types of reflection can lead to positive psychological and behavioral outcomes.

Interactive Effects of Genetic Liability and Combat Exposure on Risk of Alcohol Use Disorder Among US Service Members

Drug and Alcohol Dependence

Integrating information on genetic predisposition for alcohol use disorder with service members' history and combat exposure, we found that the effect of combat exposure on problematic alcohol consumption varies in part due to genetic vulnerability. This work helps inform more precise preventative support for service members.

A Theory of Discontinuous Positive Change in Trust Development: The Role of Critical Trust Events

This manuscript challenges the common assumption that trust grows slowly but can collapse quickly. We argue that some positive relational moments, called trust accelerators, provide unusually diagnostic information about a partner's character and can rapidly anchor trust.

Finalizing for submission to: Personnel Psychology

A Social Influence Theory of Social Undermining

This manuscript reframes social undermining as a social influence process that works through the perceptions and reactions of third-party witnesses. Integrating social influence and emotion theories, the paper examines when witnesses accept or reject the underminer's influence.

Finalizing for submission to: Journal of Applied Psychology

Bayesian Model-Average Meta-Analysis in Organizational Research

This manuscript introduces Bayesian model-average meta-analysis as a framework for strengthening knowledge synthesis in organizational research. The paper shows how Bayesian approaches can incorporate prior theoretical knowledge, represent uncertainty, and support stronger cumulative inference.

Finalizing for submission to: Academy of Management Journal

About Jason Kautz

I am an Assistant Professor of Organizational Behavior and Human Resource Management at the Jindal School of Management at the University of Texas at Dallas. My research is at the intersection of interpersonal dynamics, trust, and counterproductive work behaviors. I apply innovative methodologies to uncover the hidden patterns shaping how people work together or fall apart.

I grew up in Buffalo, New York. When I was 18, I joined the Navy and traveled the world on the aircraft carrier the USS Carl Vinson (CVN 70). Since then, I have traveled and made home in several areas.

In my free time, I enjoy cooking, gardening, and reading sci-fi novels. I am also newly discovering my passion for hiking and camping. When traveling, I prioritize trying out local coffee and visiting natural history museums when possible (dinosaurs rock!).
